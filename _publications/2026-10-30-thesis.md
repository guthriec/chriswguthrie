## Abstract

A thread of research – see, for example, [20] – has investigated a phenomenon in which the worst-case adversarial regret of certain online learning games can be evaluated (or upper bounded) by taking worst-case expectations of stochastic processes. One particularly direct and simple instance of this phenomenon was first observed by Thomas Cover in 1965 [4], in which an expectation with respect to an i.i.d. distribution completely characterizes achievable data-dependent loss or risk profiles for a learner who is trying to sequentially predict the outcomes of a binary sequence. The similarities between Cover’s work and the more complex equivalences derived from (e.g.) sequential Rademacher complexities have been noted – indeed, the authors of [7] credit Cover for inspiring their work – but the simplicity and directness of Cover’s equivalence seemed to depend on the simplicity of the binary prediction setting.

We show that this is not so. We derive (relatively) simple, direct, and non-trivial equivalences in the setting of online linear optimization. This equivalence yields several results: starting from (stochastic) martingale inequalities, the equivalence to the adversarial learning setting allows us to show both:

1. the tightness of the $\sqrt{T}$ external regret bound over the 2-dimensional Euclidean disk, and
2. the optimality of the constant 2 in AdaGrad-style adaptive regret bounds over Euclidean balls.

As part of this investigation in the OLO setting, we provide a sufficient condition (which we call spherical Lipschitzness), under which our equivalence holds, and under which we can guarantee that the constrained and unconstrained versions of the problem induce the same optimal strategy for the learner.

Beyond these specific contributions, we provide a unifying framework for a subset of these “stochastic-adversarial equivalences,” which allow for direct analysis of regret bounds via expectations of the comparator term under stationary processes. We define these equivalences in a general sense, and give conditions under which learning problems will enjoy a direct equivalence. We explore these conditions in the settings of binary prediction and online linear optimization. In the binary prediction setting, we derive new “Cover-like” results for a variety of losses.
